# REVIEW — Koe v2 launch film ("The take")

Run after `check` returned **0 errors / 0 warnings / 0 infos** across lint,
layout, runtime, motion, contrast and craft, and after two
`snapshot --review` passes (beat sheet, seam strips, 360 px phone sheet).

## The creative calls I made

- **Concept 5, told as a chain of objects.** The film's argument is that the
  product voices its own launch, so the eight narration phrases were generated by
  the live Koe instance (`scripts/generate-narration.mjs`) and the screen shows the
  job that made them. The hook line the agent typed in scene 5 *is* the line you
  are hearing in scene 1 — the same phrase, submitted as a tool call.
- **Word-level sync.** Each phrase was transcribed with Parakeet
  (`scripts/build-cues.mjs` → `scripts/cues.json`), so every title, strike,
  count and stamp lands on the word that says it, not on a guess.
- **Real product, rebuilt from its own source.** Both live captures failed with
  `EBUSY` (`capture/BLOCKED.md`, `capture2/BLOCKED.md`), so nothing was taken from
  them. Every surface — the `/mcp` hero, the HEAR IT card, the client tabs, the
  one-command block, the code panel, the limits ledger — is rebuilt from
  `mcp.html` and `DESIGN.md` at 1.3–1.8x, using the brand's own fonts
  (Source Serif 4, IBM Plex Sans, DM Mono, downloaded as woff2) and the brand's
  own palette.
- **No key on screen.** The setup command shows `koe_sk_••••••••••`; the
  snippets in `mcp.html` fill in a live key, and a real one never appears.
- **Storylines not built:** *Three negations* (the struck-through broadside) and
  *Fifteen minutes* (the countdown as the whole film). Both are in
  `STORYLINES.md`; a piece of the first survives as the tension beat's bill, and
  a piece of the second as the TTL countdown in scene 7.

## What I measured, what I only looked at

**Measured:** `check` (0/0/0 on all six passes, after fixing every finding —
type floor, unreadable lines, the proxy baseline lint, the occlusion opt-outs);
word timings from Parakeet; narration durations from `ffprobe` (34.78 s of
speech across eight phrases); the composition's 45.0 s / 1350-frame length
against the 45–60 s brief.

**Looked at:** the beat sheet, the seam strips and the 360 px phone sheet, twice.

**Nobody checked:** the sound. There is no music bed — no music source exists in
this environment (no HeyGen sign-in; the registry catalog is visual blocks only) —
so the film is narration only, and the narration was **measured, not heard**. It
was generated by the product and its word timings were verified by ASR, but no
human has listened to the mix. To add a bed: drop a licensed track in
`assets/audio/` and mount it as an `<audio id="bed" data-volume="0.14">` in
`index.html`.

## The weakest shot

Scene 6 (25.7–31.3 s, the limits ledger). The numbers land correctly on their
words and the one-oxide-per-frame rule holds, but the sheet fills top-down, so
for the first ~0.3 s the frame is a header on an empty ledger, and the third row
is missing until 3.34 s. With more time I would give the ledger a printed
watermark or a partial row already on the sheet at the cut, so the eye has
something to hold while the voice works.

## Warnings kept on purpose

None were kept: every `check` finding was fixed rather than suppressed. The only
opt-outs in the code are two `data-layout-allow-occlusion` marks on the typing
masks in scenes 3 and 4 (the mask is supposed to cover the text it reveals) and
`data-layout-allow-overflow` on each scene's camera wrapper (the 1.02–1.03 push
deliberately runs 2–3 % past the frame edge and is clipped by the root).

## If a frame looks wrong

`SPEC.md` holds the shot-by-shot plan, `STORYLINES.md` the two roads not taken,
and `scripts/generate-narration.mjs` + `scripts/build-cues.mjs` regenerate the
narration and its word timings if a line ever changes. Re-render with
`npx hyperframes@0.8.193 render`.
