# Storylines — Koe v2 launch film

Route: product launch / promo. 45–60 s, 1920×1080 @ 30 fps, 16:9, narrated by Koe
itself. `flow: automation`, `storyboard: no` → **no stops**: I take the
recommendation, build it, and name the other two below.

## A. The take (PICKED)

- **Device / form:** the result first, carried by one object — a real tool call
  travels into a real file, into a waveform, into the mark · **object-to-object
  chain**: every shot ends on the thing that opens the next.
- **What you see and feel:** the film opens on the voice you are already hearing,
  then follows a single `generate_voiceover` call from an agent session to an MP3
  sitting in a project. It feels like watching a real request succeed, quietly —
  proof rather than persuasion.
- **First two seconds:** a real frame of the live `/mcp` page — "Give your agent a
  voice." — with the HEAR IT waveform card already playing, no black, no logo.
- **Beats:** hook (the voice you're hearing) → tension (what voiceover used to
  cost) → reveal (Koe v2 at one URL) → proof (connect → call → file lands) →
  payoff (the real limits, the real retention) → claim → end card (the mark
  carried in).
- **Look:** paper ground `#f2f0e9` with 1.5% fibre noise, ink `#181613`, oxide
  `#b23a2b` rationed to one focal per frame; Source Serif 4 display, IBM Plex Sans
  body, DM Mono labels — the brand's own medium, flat surfaces and 1px rules.
- **Sound:** Koe's own edge voice as the narration (the proof is the medium), a
  low warm paper-work bed underneath that opens at the reveal, a click on the
  cursor and one riser into the mark.

## B. Three negations

- **Device / form:** type carries the argument · **cut on the beat**.
- **What you see and feel:** a printed broadside strikes through *an account*,
  *a card*, *a meter running* — then the free-tier table sets itself as a spec
  sheet. It feels declarative, poster-like, confident.
- **First two seconds:** one word filling the frame — "free" — with the strike
  rule drawing through the word it refuses.
- **Beats:** hook (the strike) → tension (the three costs) → reveal (Koe v2) →
  proof (limits table building row by row) → payoff (numbers landing) → claim →
  end card.
- **Look:** same paper/ink world, but type-led: display at 150–200px, one oxide
  word per screen, hairlines as the only structure.
- **Sound:** percussive bed cutting on the strikes; each strike a key tick, the
  table lands on a single impact.

## C. Fifteen minutes (the bold one — not picked)

- **Device / form:** a number lands — a countdown is the spine · **one
  continuous take** that never cuts away from the clock.
- **What you see and feel:** a clock is already at 14:52 when the film starts and
  runs the whole way; every scene happens inside it, and the waveform behind it
  decays as the retention window closes. It feels honest to the point of audacity
  — a product advertising its own forgetting.
- **First two seconds:** 14:52 in DM Mono at 200px, ticking, with the EPHEMERAL
  stamp half-printed.
- **Beats:** hook (the clock) → tension (what other products keep) → reveal (Koe
  keeps nothing) → proof (the job's own lifecycle: queued → generating → done →
  deleted) → payoff (the clock hits zero and the file is already saved locally) →
  claim → end card.
- **Look:** paper with a printed ledger; the countdown is the only oxide element
  in every frame; everything else is ink and hairline.
- **Sound:** near-silence under the tension, a mechanical tick on the second
  hand, the bed entering only when the local file is saved.

**Why A:** a launch film whose narration was generated by the product it
launches is evidence a viewer can hear, and the object-to-object chain lets the
real UI do the persuading. B is the most legible idea here and therefore the one
every competitor's launch video already is. C is the boldest, but it argues
retention policy when the headline is "give your agent a voice."
